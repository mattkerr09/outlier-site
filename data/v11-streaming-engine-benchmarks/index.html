Streaming engine benchmarks — Plus + Vision tok/s on Apple Silicon

Outlier · published 2026-05-09 Last updated 2026-05-20
Quick answer
  • Current engine (V9 paged): Plus 397B at ~2.1 tok/s, ~11 GB RSS, 3.6 GB Wired Memory on a 64 GB Mac.
  • Both streaming engines retired: V11 in v1.11.71, V10 in v1.11.73.
  • Why: V10/V11 per-token streaming committed 57–58 GB of Metal-wired memory during prefill — enough to crash a 32 GB Mac — for only a ~30–40% tok/s gain.
  • V9 paged (K=20 hot experts + 8 GB LRU) uses 15× less wired memory at 3.6 GB. On a footprint-first tier, that's the right trade.
Engine version note: Both streaming engines have been retired — V11 in v1.11.71 and V10 in v1.11.73 — after M1 Ultra sweeps found their per-token expert streaming committed 57–58 GB of Metal-wired memory. The current shipping engine for Plus (and the Vision default) is V9 paged inference. The retired engines' numbers are kept here for transparency; new installs use V9.

Outlier's MoE engines (V9 paged, V10 streaming, V11 hybrid) share one idea: keep active experts in unified memory, read cold experts from disk. They differ in how they cache — and that difference decided which one ships. The streaming variants were retired in favor of V9 paged because their per-token streaming pattern committed far more Metal-wired memory. This page publishes the raw comparison.

Methodology

HardwareM1 Ultra Mac Studio, 64 GB unified memory, iogpu.wired_limit_mb=57344
Model versionsPlus = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B MLX 4-bit; Vision = Qwen3.6-35B-A3B MLX 4-bit
Inference stackmlx-lm 0.31.3, MLX 0.18.x
Aggregation5-run mean per configuration, fresh process per run (no warm-cache bias)
Soak runs50-turn multi-turn workloads on Plus; 20-turn on Vision
RAM measurementOS-level RSS via ps -o rss=, sampled per token
Date range2026-05-07 (V10 results), 2026-05-09 (V11 soak verdict), 2026-05-19 (V11 retired, v1.11.71), v1.11.73 (V10 retired)

The decisive column is Wired Memory. V10 and V11's per-token expert streaming committed 57–58 GB of Metal command-buffer memory during prefill — enough to crash a 32 GB Mac on the first generation — to buy a ~30–40% tok/s gain over V9. V9 paged holds 3.6 GB Wired (15× less) at 2.1 tok/s. For a tier whose entire purpose is fitting a 209 GB model on a small Mac, footprint beats speed, so V9 ships.

Vision keeps a V10 opt-in for users on tighter RAM (lower footprint, ~6.6 tok/s), but the default is V9 at 16.31 tok/s on 24 GB+ Macs. The earlier 16 GB-MacBook-Air result depended on V11's LRU specifically; with V11 retired, Vision's practical floor is 24 GB.

Soak verdict — what passed and what didn't

From the 2026-05-09 soak verdict on V11 across 9 cycles:

  • 135/135 turns coherent — no output collapse, no repetition loops.
  • 0 crashes, 0 segfaults across both Plus N=4 (100 turns) and Vision N=2 (35 turns).
  • Engine-swap V11→V9→V11 round-trip: post-swap tok/s 1.51 vs pre-swap 1.36 — no regression.
  • Idle-resume across 5-minute sleep: clean (no orphan threads, no leaked tensors).
  • Memory pressure on 50-turn Plus: 14.34 GB peak vs smoke 7.34 GB — the regression that drove retirement.

How to reproduce

  1. Install Outlier (Pro tier or Founders for Plus 397B): outlier.host
  2. Open Settings → Engine Mode → Plus runs on Capacity (V9 paged). Any stored V10/V11 preference auto-migrates to Capacity.
  3. Run a multi-prompt set; record tok/s from the response footer.
  4. Sample OS RSS with ps -o pid,rss,command -p $(pgrep -f outlier-cli) during generation.

For V10/V11 reproduction (historical): the source-level implementations live in repo history before commit 525298f (v1.11.71) and the v1.11.73 V10 removal. Neither ships in current DMGs.

Frequently asked questions

What engine does Outlier use for Plus now?

V9 paged inference (K=20 hot experts per layer plus an 8 GB LRU cache). It runs Plus 397B at about 2.1 tok/s with ~11 GB RSS and 3.6 GB Wired Memory. The V10 and V11 streaming engines were retired.

Why were the streaming engines retired?

V10 and V11 per-token expert streaming committed 57–58 GB of Metal-wired memory during prefill — enough to crash a 32 GB Mac — for only a 30–40% tok/s gain. V9 paged uses 3.6 GB Wired (15× less), so on a footprint-first tier it wins.

How were the numbers measured?

5-run mean per configuration, fresh process per run, on an M1 Ultra with OS-level RSS sampled per token.
