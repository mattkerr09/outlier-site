How to run Qwen3.5-397B on a Mac without 512 GB of RAM

Outlier · published 2026-05-19 Last updated 2026-05-20
Quick answer
  • MoE architecture activates only 10 of 512 experts per token.
  • Paged expert streaming keeps active experts in RAM, reads the rest from SSD.
  • Result: 11 GB peak RAM, 2.1 tok/s on a 64 GB Mac Studio Ultra.
  • Internal NVMe SSD (5–15 GB/s read) is required; external Thunderbolt SSDs are ~2× slower.

Qwen3.5-397B-A17B is a Mixture-of-Experts model from Alibaba. At MLX 4-bit quantization the full weights are about 209 GB. The standard advice is "you need a server" — and for a 70B dense model that's right. For Qwen's MoE architecture there's a way around it: keep the active experts in unified memory and stream the rest from your SSD on demand.

The model in one paragraph

Qwen3.5-397B-A17B has 512 experts per layer. For each token, a router picks 8 of them and only those 10 do work. "397B" is the total parameter count across all experts; "A17B" is the count active per token. The router decision is content-dependent — different tokens light up different experts. Over a long conversation, expert usage is reasonably stable, which is what makes caching viable.

Why a 64 GB Mac is enough

If you only need 10 of 512 experts active per token, you don't need all 512 resident in RAM. You need:

  • The shared backbone (attention layers, embedding, router weights) — small fraction of total size, always resident.
  • A cache of recently-used experts — sized to fit your available RAM.
  • An on-disk store of all 512 experts, ready to be read into the cache on a miss.

On a 64 GB Mac Studio Ultra running Outlier's V9 paged engine, peak OS-level RSS during Plus 397B generation is about 11 GB. The remaining ~50 GB of RAM is the OS plus your other apps. Generation speed is about 2.1 tokens per second. The bottleneck is not memory; it's how fast the SSD can read expert weights when the router asks for one that's not in the cache.

What the SSD needs to be

Apple's first-party Mac Studio SSDs sustain several GB/s of sequential read. A first-generation M1 Ultra reads at ~7 GB/s; M2 Ultra and M3 Max are higher. That's enough to keep up with the expert demand for one decoded token per ~340 ms — exactly the regime that produces ~2.1 tok/s.

Two implications:

  • External Thunderbolt SSDs are slower (typically 2–3 GB/s sustained). Plus on a Thunderbolt SSD works but slower; expect roughly half the tok/s.
  • If your internal SSD is nearly full and macOS is fragmenting writes, you can see read regressions. Keep 100+ GB free.

The honest tradeoff

Compared to the capacity engine (the path that keeps everything in RAM), V9 paged inference uses about 10× less peak memory in exchange for being SSD-bound instead of memory-bound. On long contexts where expert usage stabilizes, the cache holds the hot set and SSD reads drop. On a fresh prompt with cold experts, every token triggers cache misses and reads, so the first response is the slowest.

Compared to cloud inference, you're paying ~30× speed (2.1 tok/s vs ~80–100 from Claude Opus) for: no API key, no rate limit, no cloud trip with your code, no monthly bill after the model is on disk. For an architecture review, a long debugging conversation, or a deep code refactor, that tradeoff often comes out in favor of local. For "what's the syntax for X in Python" it doesn't.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need 512 GB of RAM to run Qwen3.5-397B?

No. With paged expert streaming the model runs on a 64 GB Mac at about 2.1 tok/s and roughly 11 GB peak RAM.

Where are the model weights stored?

On your SSD as MLX 4-bit safetensors, about 209 GB. Experts stream into memory on demand as the router selects them.

Does SSD speed matter?

Yes. Internal Apple Silicon NVMe sustaining 5 to 15 GB/s is needed; external Thunderbolt SSDs are roughly half the throughput and run correspondingly slower.
