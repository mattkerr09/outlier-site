A handful of local AI tools for Mac really are free. No subscription, and the thing runs on your own hardware. Ollama and Jan are the free-and-open-source ones. Outlier and LM Studio are free to start and charge for the upgrades. Below I break down what 'free' actually buys you with each, so you don't pick the wrong one.

What 'free' means for each

  • Free and open source: Ollama (MIT) and Jan (Apache 2.0). Both are free to run on your Mac; Ollama also sells optional cloud plans.
  • Free tier, paid upgrades: Outlier (free Nano + Lite) and LM Studio, whose app is free and which sells optional hosted plans.
  • All four run local models on your Mac. No usage caps, and they work offline.

Which free option to pick

  • Want open-source with a friendly UI? Jan.
  • Want a clean chat GUI with a big model catalog? LM Studio.
  • Want maximum flexibility and true open source? Ollama. A huge library of open models plus GGUF files from Hugging Face, scriptable, on macOS, Windows and Linux.
  • Want a Mac app with a suggested model? Outlier's free tier. It picks a model for your memory. Download that model before your first chat. No account.

What the free tiers can actually do

Nano uses Qwen3.5-4B. Its model download is 2.37 GB, with a 6 GB memory minimum, so it fits an 8 GB Mac. Lite uses Qwen3.5-9B. It downloads 5.04 GB and needs at least 12 GB of memory; 16 GB gives it more room. These are the models and requirements in Outlier's model list. Intel Macs are not supported.

Both free models can answer everyday questions, help draft text and summarize short passages you give them. Nano also handles small shell scripts, but check any code before running it. Hard coding tasks and long documents can need a larger model. Nano and Lite are free forever with no account. Pro adds Quick, Core, Vision 3.8 and Plus. The pricing page has the current amounts.

Start a free local chat in Outlier

  1. Get the app from the download page. Open the DMG and drag Outlier to Applications. The install guide shows how to check your chip, memory and free disk space.
  2. Open Outlier and download a free model. The first screen suggests one for your Mac. Press Download & start and let the model download finish. This needs an internet connection, separate from the app download.
  3. Type a question. Try asking it to rewrite a short note you paste into chat. Read the reply and check that it kept your meaning.
  4. Try local chat with Wi-Fi off. After the model has loaded, disconnect any other internet connection too. Ask a new question and check whether the reply arrives. This is a check you can do on your own Mac.

To add the other free model later, open Settings > Models, click Download and wait for the green check before switching tiers in chat. The model download guide explains those controls.

The model and any files must already be on your Mac before you go offline. A cloud-only file still needs to be downloaded. Web search and cloud models need a connection; choose a downloaded local model for this check. The offline guide covers what you can use with Wi-Fi off.

The honest catch with 'free'

Free isn't free of everything. It still eats your Mac's RAM, disk, and battery, and a long session on a big model will pull real power off the wall. What you don't get is a subscription, a per-token bill, or your data leaving the machine. For most people that's a trade worth making.
