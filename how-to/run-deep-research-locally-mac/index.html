How to run deep research locally on your Mac

Outlier · solo-built in Grand Rapids · published 2026-08-14 Last updated 2026-08-14
Quick answer
  • Deep research is a multi-step job: plan the question, read several sources, then write up what they actually said.
  • The reasoning runs on your Mac; only the page fetches touch the network.
  • Answers carry citations, because research you can't check is just confident prose.
  • It takes minutes, not seconds — that is what separates it from a single search.

A one-shot answer to a research question is a guess with good grammar. Real research means deciding what to look up, reading more than one source, noticing where they disagree, and showing your work.

What makes it "deep"

A normal answer is one pass: your question in, a paragraph out. Deep research is a loop — decompose the question, retrieve, read, notice what's missing, retrieve again, then synthesise.

The synthesis is where value is added or destroyed. A summary that drops the citation is worse than no summary, because it launders a claim into something that looks settled. Keeping sources attached is the whole discipline.

Running one

  1. Ask a real question — one that genuinely needs several sources.
  2. Let it work. You'll see it progress through the steps.
  3. Read the write-up with its sources and follow the ones that matter.

Give it something with substance. "What's the capital of France" wastes the machinery; "how do these four approaches actually compare" is what it's for.

Being straight about the limits

  • It reads the web, so the web's errors are available to it. Citations exist so you can catch that.
  • Page fetching needs a connection. The thinking is local; the reading isn't.
  • A local model is not a frontier model. On a genuinely hard synthesis you should read the sources yourself — the citations make that possible rather than theoretical.
  • It takes minutes. If it returned instantly it didn't do the work.

Common questions

Can a local AI do deep research?

Yes — planning, retrieval across multiple sources, and a written synthesis with citations. The reasoning happens on your Mac; the page fetches use the network.

Does deep research work offline?

The reasoning does, but reading current web pages needs a connection. Offline, the model is limited to what it already knows and what you give it.

Are the sources real?

Citations point at pages actually retrieved during the run, so you can open them and check. That is exactly why they're there.

How long does it take?

Minutes rather than seconds — it is doing several rounds of retrieval and reading, not one.
