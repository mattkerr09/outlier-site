How to run local AI on an Apple silicon Mac

Matt Kerr · Outlier · published 2026-06-09 Last updated 2026-11-09
Quick answer

You need a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer) on macOS 15 or later. Its memory (RAM) decides how big a model it can run. Then there are three steps:

  1. Download the app. The 1.11.1006 DMG is 277 MB, about 22 seconds on a 100 Mbps connection.
  2. Open it. Drag it to Applications and double-click. On a fresh virtual Mac running macOS 15, it opened within 20 seconds.
  3. Pick the model it suggests. The app reads your RAM and names one: Nano (2.37 GB) on an 8 GB Mac, Lite (5.04 GB) on 16 GB or more. That download takes about 3 to 7 minutes at 100 Mbps, once.

After that it works with Wi-Fi off. There's no account and no API key, and it's free to start.

What your Mac needs

  • Apple silicon. Any M-series chip, M1 or newer. Intel Macs can't run it. To check, open the Apple menu, choose About This Mac and read the Chip line.
  • macOS 15 or later. It's a free update, and every Apple silicon Mac can install it.
  • Enough RAM for the model. 8 GB runs Nano. 16 GB runs Lite comfortably. 24 GB is where the 27B models start. About This Mac shows it on the Memory line.
  • Free disk. About 3 GB to start with Nano, about 6 GB with Lite. Each bigger model adds its own download.

How fast. Speed is counted in tokens per second (a token is a word or a piece of one). In the 1.11.1006 release check (November 2026, the median of three runs on an M1 Ultra Mac Studio with 64 GB), Nano wrote 96.7 tok/s, Lite 69.4 and Core 24.5. The April 2026 benchmark on the same Mac had 71.7, 53.4 and 20.7, so plan on about 20 to 25 tok/s for Core. A MacBook Air has far less memory bandwidth than an M1 Ultra, so expect lower numbers on one.

Step by step, with times

  1. Download (about 22 seconds). Press Download free on this page. You get a signed, notarized .dmg. The 1.11.1006 file is 277 MB (276,975,098 bytes), about 22 seconds at 100 Mbps.
  2. Install (a few seconds). Open the DMG and drag Outlier into your Applications folder.
  3. Open it (within 20 seconds). macOS may ask once whether to open an app from the internet. Apple has notarized it, so click Open. On the fresh test Mac it was up within 20 seconds.
  4. Pick the suggested model (3 to 7 minutes, once). The first screen shows your chip and RAM, names a model and gives you one Download & start button, with a link to pick a different one. Nano is 2.37 GB, about 3 minutes at 100 Mbps. Lite is 5.04 GB, about 7 minutes. This is the only step that needs internet.
  5. Chat. Type a question. The answer is written on your Mac, with no account and no key.

The download times are arithmetic, not measurements: the file size over a 100 Mbps line. A slower line takes longer in proportion.

Confirm it's actually local

Once the model has downloaded, turn off Wi-Fi and send a prompt. It still answers. Switching between models you've downloaded works offline too, and so does project memory if you have Pro.

Adding bigger models

The free Nano and Lite handle everyday questions, writing and summaries. For serious coding or long documents, Core 27B is part of Pro, wants a 24 GB Mac or bigger, and downloads itself the first time you pick it. The biggest, Plus 397B, is Pro too and needs a 64 GB Mac. Bigger models mean bigger downloads, so watch your disk space.

Frequently asked questions

Which local AI model should I run on my Mac?

The biggest one that fits fully in your RAM, and the app suggests it for you: Nano on an 8 GB Mac, Lite on 16 GB or more, and Core on 24 GB or more with Pro. The table above lists each model's download size and minimum RAM.

Can an 8 GB Mac run local AI?

Yes. Nano, a 4B model, runs fully in memory on an 8 GB Mac, and it's free. Lite and the bigger models need more memory than an 8 GB Mac has.

Do I need an account to use it?

No. The free tier works with no account and no API key. Pro needs a licence key from your purchase, activated online once.

Why does the first launch need internet?

Only to download the model weights once. After the model is on your disk, the app runs fully offline: no connection needed for chat or agent work.

Will it run on an Intel Mac?

No. Outlier requires Apple silicon (M1 or newer) because it uses Apple's MLX framework and the Mac's unified memory. Intel Macs are not supported.
