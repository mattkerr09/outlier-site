Outlier vs Perplexity: local Mac AI vs a cloud answer engine

Matt Kerr · Outlier · published 2026-07-23 Updated 2026-07-23
Quick answer

Perplexity is a cloud answer engine: it runs live web searches and returns a cited answer, but every query leaves your machine, it needs a connection, and Pro is a subscription. Outlier runs models entirely on your own Apple Silicon Mac — no account, no usage caps, nothing uploaded, works with Wi-Fi off. Outlier's free tier has no web search at all; Outlier Pro adds opt-in web research and Deep Research, where only the query leaves your Mac. Perplexity's retrieval is still always on, indexed far more broadly, and cited inline. Asking about this week's news? Perplexity. Asking about documents you can't send to a server? Outlier.

I'm Matt Kerr, and I built Outlier — so treat this as a comparison written by an interested party. I've tried to be accurate rather than flattering, and where Perplexity is clearly better I say so. These two get compared constantly, but they solve different problems. One is a search product with a language model attached. The other is a language model running on your own hardware, with search as an optional extra you can switch off.

What Perplexity is

Perplexity calls itself an "answer engine." You ask a question, it runs live web searches, feeds the retrieved pages to large language models, and returns a synthesized answer with inline citations you can click. The models are a mix: Perplexity's own Sonar, built on Llama 3.3 70B and further trained for search, plus third-party frontier models routed through its Agent API — Claude, GPT-5, Gemini and Grok among them.

It runs nearly everywhere: web, iOS 18+, iPadOS, Android, a native macOS app called Personal Computer, the Comet browser on macOS and Windows, and a developer API. There's no official CLI, but an MCP server (npx -y @perplexity-ai/mcp-server) drops search and research tools into editors like Claude Code and Cursor. Deep Research runs dozens of searches across hundreds of sources and returns a cited report in two to four minutes; Model Council, a Max feature, sends one question to three frontier models and reconciles where they agree and disagree.

What Outlier is

Outlier is a native Mac app for Apple Silicon (M1 or later, macOS 12+). The model runs on your own chip. No account, no login, no usage cap, nothing uploaded — turn Wi-Fi off and keep working.

Six tiers ship in the app, from Nano 4B (2.37 GB, 6 GB RAM, 71.7 tok/s on an M1 Ultra) through Core 27B up to Plus 397B-a17b, which streams its experts from SSD and peaks near 11 GB RSS on a 64 GB Mac (209 GB on disk, 128 GB recommended) at about 1.59 tok/s on an M1 Ultra. Nano and Lite are free with no account; Pro is a one-time purchase for all seven.

Core 27B matched Claude Opus on 98.9% of rubric checks across a 54-prompt head-to-head, and 100% on the nine hardest. Core 27B scores 0.866 on HumanEval, and on a blind slice of SWE-bench Verified the local 27B measured about 45%.

Search is where the two products actually meet. The free tiers have no web search: turning it on returns a Pro paywall. Outlier Pro ships web research and Deep Research — a toggle you can flip per conversation, and when it's on only the query leaves your Mac, not your prompt history, your files or the model's context. Web search goes to DuckDuckGo, Mojeek and Wikipedia; Deep Research adds Crossref, OpenAlex, StackExchange and Hacker News. It is a much smaller retrieval stack than Perplexity's, it isn't always on, and it doesn't render the inline click-through citations Perplexity built its product around.

Where Perplexity genuinely wins

Several of these aren't close, and pretending otherwise would waste your time.

  • Live web grounding with citations. This is the whole product, not a bolt-on. Retrieval is always on, runs against a far broader index than the three engines Outlier queries, and every claim renders as a numbered inline citation you can click and check mid-sentence. Outlier Pro's web research is real but comparatively thin: a three-engine lookup rather than a crawled index of its own, no citation UX of that quality, and a local 27B doing the synthesis instead of a frontier model.
  • Measured citation accuracy. Columbia's Tow Center, March 2025, tested eight AI search tools across 1,600 queries. Free Perplexity had the lowest error rate of the eight at 37% incorrect, ahead of ChatGPT Search, Gemini, Copilot, DeepSeek and Grok-3, which was worst at 94%. Worth knowing before you upgrade: the study ran Perplexity and Perplexity Pro as separate entries, and Perplexity Pro — the $9/month tier this page compares against — scored worse than the free one in the same run.
  • Many frontier models on one subscription. Per-thread model selection across the Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok families, instead of paying four vendors.
  • Deep Research on frontier models, and Model Council. Outlier Pro has its own Deep Research, but the synthesis is done by a local 27B on your laptop's power budget; Perplexity's runs on frontier models and someone else's GPUs, which is a different quality of write-up on a hard question. Model Council — one question to three frontier models, with agreements and disagreements reconciled — has no local equivalent: Outlier can run a side-by-side compare, but only across its own tiers unless you bring your own API keys and pay those vendors directly.
  • It's everywhere. Phone, tablet, browser, desktop, API. Outlier is one Mac.
  • Enterprise posture. SOC 2 Type II, audit logs, SCIM provisioning, configurable retention and usage analytics on Enterprise Max — things an unmanaged local app doesn't provide.
  • It gives back. An MIT-licensed MCP server, Apache-2.0 GPU kernels and SDKs, and R1-1776, the open-weights post-trained DeepSeek-R1 it open-sourced in February 2025.

Outlier also has no image generation, no always-on voice conversation mode (it has push-to-record voice input and read-aloud, not a hands-free spoken back-and-forth), no mobile app, no Windows or Linux build, and no cloud sync. If any of those are on your list, this comparison ends here.

What it costs over a year

Perplexity Pro is $17/month when billed annually, read from perplexity.ai/pro on 14 August 2026; Max is listed at $249. Enterprise Pro is $40/user/month ($400/year) and Enterprise Max $325/user/month ($3,250/year). Third-party trackers report discounted annual plans and a $10/month education tier, but I couldn't confirm those — perplexity.ai/pricing returns HTTP 403 to automated fetching — so treat them as unconfirmed.

Outlier Pro is a one-time purchase — $12.42/month. Founders Lifetime is $249 once. Nano and Lite stay free.

The API is priced separately and is reasonable: Sonar at $1 per million input and output tokens, Sonar Pro at $3/$15, plus request fees of $5–$14 per thousand depending on search context size. The Search API bills $5.00 per 1,000 requests, and third-party models routed through the Agent API carry no Perplexity markup.

Free-tier quotas aren't documented on any page I could reach. Perplexity says free users get "access to a limited number of answers per day" for Deep Research; the numbers people quote come from third-party trackers that disagree with each other.

What happens to your data

With Perplexity, every query goes to Perplexity's servers — not a criticism, just how a search product has to work. Even the Mac Personal Computer app, which reaches local files and 400+ connectors, orchestrates all of it "in a secure development sandbox on Perplexity servers," by Perplexity's own description. Local file content can travel to the cloud, and I haven't found a published boundary of what stays on device.

The privacy policy permits using collected data to "Improve or create services and products, including our AI models." The only training carve-out written into it is email content. The policy also states plainly that Perplexity does not sell personal data or send queries to advertisers. Guides widely describe a data-retention toggle that's on by default and must be switched off in Settings; I couldn't verify that from a primary source, since the Help Center is behind a 403.

Comet's design is better than it gets credit for — history, cookies, passwords and card details stay on device, and only the current tab plus relevant history is sent when you invoke the Assistant. Against that, Brave's security team documented indirect prompt injection in Comet: page content reached the model without separating user instructions from untrusted webpage text, letting hidden instructions exfiltrate data from logged-in sessions. Brave reported it 25 July 2025 and, after the 20 August 2025 disclosure, said Perplexity "still hasn't fully mitigated the kind of attack described here."

Two more things a careful buyer should know: the iOS App Store privacy label discloses precise location, purchase history and device identifiers collected and linked to your identity; and there's real legal overhang, including copyright suits from several publishers and an Amazon suit over Comet's shopping agent that produced a March 2026 injunction blocking the agent from Amazon.

Outlier's version of this section is short. Inference is local, so prompts, files and chat history stay on the machine. No account, no server-side log, no training toggle to find, because there's no server. The one deliberate exception is Pro's web research: switch it on and that turn's search query goes to DuckDuckGo, Mojeek and Wikipedia — the query only, not your prompt history or documents — and the app names every destination in its own settings before it sends anything. Switch it off and nothing leaves. Six tiers use Apache 2.0 base models; Quick ships under the Gemma Terms of Use, and weights are published at huggingface.co/Outlier-Ai. Sonar's weights are not published, and Perplexity's Terms of Service assign all rights to the company and prohibit reverse engineering.

Who should pick which

Pick Perplexity if your questions are mostly about the current world — news, prices, releases, anything past a training cutoff. Outlier Pro can reach the web too, but if research is the job rather than an occasional lookup, Perplexity's broader index, inline citations and frontier-model synthesis are worth the subscription. Pick it if you need answers on a phone or on Windows, or if a team needs SOC 2 and SCIM.

Pick Outlier if the material can't leave your machine — client files, patient notes, unreleased source code, legal drafts. Pick it if you work on planes or in dead zones, if usage caps have interrupted you mid-task, or if you'd rather pay $249 once than $240 a year.

Plenty of people run both, and that's the honest recommendation for most knowledge workers. Perplexity for serious research across the live web; Outlier for anything you wouldn't paste into a website, plus the occasional lookup its own search can cover. The overlap is narrower than the feature lists suggest.
