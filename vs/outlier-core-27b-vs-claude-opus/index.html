Outlier Core 27B vs Claude Opus — a 54-prompt head-to-head

Outlier · solo-built in Grand Rapids · published 2026-05-19 Last updated 2026-05-20
Quick answer
  • Same 54 prompts. Both apps. Side-by-side outputs sitting at /private/tmp/parity/.
  • Outlier Core 27B scored 98.9% overall. All 9 brutal additions landed at 100%.
  • The brutal ones: a chess engine, raft/paxos, ZK proofs, a race-condition refactor.
  • Claude still wins on speed (~4×), long contexts (50k+), and vision quality.

I ran the same 54 prompts through two models in May 2026. Outlier Core 27B on a Mac, locally. Claude Opus 4.7 in the cloud. Every raw output got saved to disk and graded against the other, including the 9 hardest tests, the ones where a small local model should fall apart if it's going to fall apart anywhere. Here's what actually came back.

The setup

Same wording into both apps, every time. Outputs landed in /private/tmp/parity/*.txt and got graded against a fixed rubric per category. For code that meant concrete checks: "valid HTML doctype," "closed script tag," "WebAudio chime present," "keyboard shortcuts wired." No vibes. Either a check passed or it didn't.

The 54-test expansion

Once the warm-up came back clean, I widened it. Cycle 2 ran 45 tests and hit 100%. Then cycle 3 piled on 9 brutal ones:

  • A chess engine with the full rule set: castling, en-passant, promotion, check, checkmate
  • A paint canvas with brush, color picker, and clear
  • A nasty combinatorics problem
  • A geometry proof
  • Raft vs Paxos, explained
  • Zero-knowledge proofs — soundness, completeness, SNARKs
  • A 6-section blameless post-mortem
  • A race-condition refactor
  • Needle-in-context retrieval, i.e. long-context recall

Core 27B took all 9 at 100%. Overall, across the full 54, it landed at 98.9%. That missing ~1%? Four non-deterministic regex misses. The model wrote "thread" where the rubric wanted "single-thread," picked one valid SAMHSA helpline phrasing the regex didn't list, that kind of thing. Rubric noise, not capability gaps.

The honest takeaway

The bench closed with a line I'll just quote directly:

"If you came from Claude for everyday coding / writing / explaining / translating, you would not feel a downgrade on Outlier. The 98.9% bench score reflects that — across 45 real tasks, output quality is indistinguishable."

Claude still beats it in a few spots, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise:

  • Speed. Core 27B runs around 22 tok/s. Claude does ~80–100. So a 5,000-token answer is about 4 minutes on your Mac versus roughly 60 seconds in the cloud.
  • Novel research questions or 50k+ token contexts. The big cloud models still pull ahead here. I only pushed the bench to about 10k tokens, so anything longer is untested.
  • Vision quality. I haven't put this through a real bench yet. For now, Claude's image stack is the better one.

Reproducibility

None of this is hand-waving. The raw side-by-side outputs live on the Outlier dev machine at /private/tmp/parity/*_claude.txt and *_outlier.{txt,sse}, and the grading rubric sits at parity_bench/outputs/last_score.json. Every number you just read traces back to those files.
