Notion AI vs local AI: what you give up, what you gain

Matt Kerr · Outlier · published 2026-07-23 Updated 2026-07-23
Quick answer

Notion AI is cloud AI built into a workspace: it reads your Notion pages and connected apps, runs on frontier models hosted by Notion and by providers like OpenAI and Anthropic, and is included only on Notion's Business and Enterprise plans. Outlier runs the model on your own Mac — no account, no upload, no usage caps, works with Wi-Fi off — but has no workspace, no teammates, no connectors, and no scheduled agents. If your work lives in Notion and your team edits it together, Notion AI does a job a local chat app isn't built to do; if a document can't leave the machine at all, only on-device inference gets you there.

Disclosure first: I built Outlier, so read the parts where I praise Notion carefully and the parts where I praise my own app skeptically. I've tried to write the comparison I'd want if I were the one choosing — including the several places where Notion AI is simply the better tool.

What Notion AI actually is

It isn't a chatbot with a Notion skin. It's a set of features stitched into the workspace app: a chat agent that answers over your pages, writing and summarizing tools in the editor, database autofill, AI Meeting Notes for transcription, and Enterprise Search across connected third-party apps. The AI already knows your corpus because your corpus is the product.

The models aren't Notion's. Its security documentation says Notion "currently utilizes various large language models (LLMs) hosted by Notion as well as by organizations such as Anthropic and OpenAI." As of the 3.6 release on July 1, 2026, you can choose GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.5 or 4.8, Gemini 3, Grok 4.3, or the open-weight GLM 5.2, or let an "Auto" setting route per task. That release also added External Agents — Claude and Cursor first — assignable like teammates.

Notion's privacy posture is better than the category average, and it's written down. By default neither Notion nor its AI subprocessors use customer data to train models, and Notion says its contracts with those subprocessors prohibit it. Training is opt-in through a named program, AI LEAP. Retention is where the tiers diverge: providers use zero data retention for Enterprise workspaces, while for non-Enterprise ones they "only retain Customer Data for 30 days or fewer." One documented exception — certain features can use data-retaining LLMs, off by default, which an admin can switch on workspace-wide.

What Outlier is

A native Mac app for Apple Silicon (M1 or later, macOS 12+). It downloads a model once and then runs inference on your own GPU — no account, no login, no usage caps, and it keeps working with Wi-Fi off. Seven tiers run from Nano (4B, 2.37 GB, fits in 6 GB of RAM) up to Plus (397B-a17b, 209 GB on disk, streaming experts from SSD at roughly 11 GB peak RSS). Nano and Lite are free without an account; Pro is $20/month or $149/year.

What it doesn't have, plainly: no live web search, no image generation, no voice mode, no mobile app, no Windows or Linux build, no cloud sync. Several of those gaps matter here.

Where Notion AI is clearly better

This section isn't a formality. There are whole categories of work where I'd tell you to use Notion and not my app.

  • Top-end model quality. A quantized 27B model on a laptop does not match Claude Opus 4.8 or GPT-5.2 on the hardest reasoning. Notion hands you those models directly, and lets you switch providers without losing workspace context.
  • It already knows your work. The AI runs over your pages, docs, tasks, and databases with no indexing setup and no picking files before every question. Outlier only knows what you put in front of it.
  • Cross-app retrieval. Enterprise Search spans Slack, Google Drive, GitHub, Outlook Mail and Calendar, Microsoft file formats, and MCP connections to Mercury, Mixpanel, Miro, Box, and ClickHouse. Outlier has none of that.
  • Agents that run while you're asleep. Custom Agents fire on schedules and triggers on Notion's infrastructure. An on-device app can't run a 6 a.m. daily brief when the Mac is closed — that's the physics of local AI, not a bug in it.
  • Multiplayer output. Results land where teammates already work. Outlier's land in one person's window.
  • AI Meeting Notes. Transcription in 16 languages, speaker labeling (English only), mobile capture that survives switching apps, no bot joining the call. Outlier doesn't transcribe at all.
  • Compliance and reach. SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA with a BAA on Enterprise — plus Windows, iOS, and Android clients, a REST API, an official CLI, and an MIT-licensed MCP server. If procurement needs a signed BAA, or half your team is on Windows, Outlier isn't in the running.

What running locally actually buys you

The content never leaves. Notion's default of not training on your data is a policy backed by contracts, and I've no reason to doubt it — but it's still a promise about what a third party does with content you sent them. With Outlier there's no request for anyone to make a promise about. That stops being philosophical the moment you're handling an unsigned deal, a patient note, or a client's source code — and it matters more below Enterprise, where Notion's own docs say providers retain customer data for up to 30 days rather than zero.

It works with the network off. Notion's offline mode covers viewing and editing cached pages; anything needing a live connection is unavailable, and Notion documents no on-device model and lists no AI feature as offline-capable. On a plane, behind a firewall that blocks outbound traffic, or during a provider outage, Outlier keeps generating because the weights are on your SSD.

The cost stops moving. Notion AI comes with Business, but the automation layer is metered on top: Custom Agents have consumed Notion Credits since May 4, 2026 at $10 per 1,000 credits, and Workers begin requiring credits on October 15, 2026 at roughly $0.0023 per run. Notion's published per-run estimates span a 10× range — about $0.03–$0.11 for a Q&A agent up to $0.10–$0.30 for a daily brief — because cost scales with content read, steps run, frequency, and model chosen. By my arithmetic, one daily-brief agent lands between $3 and $9 a month on top of seats. Not expensive; just variable. Outlier's marginal cost per generation is zero.

And it's good enough for the writing half. Summarize this, tighten this, pull the action items out of these notes — a local 27B handles that. In a 54-prompt head-to-head against Claude Opus, Outlier's Core 27B matched on 98.9% of rubric checks, and on all nine of the hardest prompts. On a blind slice of SWE-bench Verified it measured about 45% (18/40) — respectable, clearly not frontier. Comparable on ordinary prose, behind on the hardest reasoning, missing everything that comes from being wired into a workspace.

Who should pick which

Stay with Notion AI if your team runs on Notion, you need output in a shared space, you want scheduled agents, you rely on transcription or cross-app search, you're on Windows or Linux or mobile, or your org needs the compliance paperwork. Nothing local replaces that stack.

Add a local model if some slice of your work can't be sent to a third party at all, you want AI on a plane or behind a firewall, you're one operator paying a per-seat price for one seat, or you'd rather have a fixed cost than a metered one. Testing that costs nothing — Nano and Lite need no account.

Run both, which is what I'd expect most people to do. Notion AI for the collaborative, connected, scheduled work; a local model for the documents that shouldn't leave the building. These two aren't really competing for the same hour of your day.
