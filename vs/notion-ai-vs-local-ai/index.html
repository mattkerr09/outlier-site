Notion AI vs local AI: what you give up, what you gain

Matt Kerr · Outlier · published 2026-07-23 Updated 2026-07-23
Quick answer

Notion AI is cloud AI built into a workspace: it reads your Notion pages and connected apps, runs on frontier models hosted by Notion and by providers like OpenAI and Anthropic, and is included only on the Business and Enterprise plans. Outlier runs the model on your own Mac — no account, no upload, no usage caps, works with Wi-Fi off — but has no workspace, teammates, connectors, or scheduled agents. If your work lives in Notion, Notion AI does a job a local chat app isn't built to do; if a document can't leave the machine, only on-device inference gets you there.

Disclosure first: I built Outlier, so read the praise of Notion carefully and the praise of my own app skeptically. These are adjacent categories — one's a workspace feature, the other's a model on your laptop — and people compare them mostly because both land near $9 a month.

What Notion AI actually is

It isn't a chatbot with a Notion skin. It's a set of features stitched into the workspace app: a chat agent that answers over your pages, writing tools in the editor, database autofill, AI Meeting Notes, Custom Agents that fire on triggers and schedules, and Enterprise Search across connected apps.

The frontier models aren't Notion's own. Its security documentation says Notion uses large language models hosted by Notion as well as by organizations such as Anthropic and OpenAI. As of the 3.6 release (July 1, 2026) you can pick GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.5 or 4.8, Gemini 3, Grok 4.3, or the open-weight GLM 5.2 — or let "Auto" route per task — and switch models mid-task.

Pricing trips people up. Notion's help center is blunt: Notion AI is only available on Business and Enterprise plans; Free and Plus get a small complimentary allowance for trying it. There's no standalone AI add-on on the pricing page, so the entry price is Business — listed at $9 per member per month — times every seat. (Plus is listed at $10, with a "Save up to 20% with yearly" note and no separate monthly figures.)

The privacy posture is better than the category average, and it's documented. By default neither Notion nor its AI subprocessors use customer data to train models, and Notion says its contracts prohibit it; training is opt-in via a program called AI LEAP. Retention splits by tier — zero with providers on Enterprise, 30 days or fewer below it — and some features can use data-retaining LLMs, off by default.

What Outlier is

A native Mac app for Apple Silicon (M1 or later, macOS 12+). It downloads a model once, then runs inference on your own GPU — no account, no usage caps, and it keeps working with Wi-Fi off. Seven tiers run from Nano (4B, 2.37 GB, fits in 6 GB of RAM) to Plus (397B-a17b, 209 GB, needs a 64 GB Mac, streaming experts from SSD at about 11 GB peak RSS). Nano and Lite are free; Pro is a one-time purchase.

What it doesn't have: no image generation, no voice mode, no mobile app, no Windows or Linux build, no cloud sync, and no connectors into Notion, Slack, or Drive. Several of those gaps matter here.

Where Notion AI is clearly better

There are whole categories of work where I'd tell you to use Notion, not my app.

  • Top-end model quality. A quantized 27B on a laptop doesn't match Claude Opus 4.8 or GPT-5.2 on the hardest reasoning. Notion hands you those models and lets you switch between them mid-task.
  • It already knows your work. The AI runs over your pages, docs, tasks, and databases — no indexing setup, no picking files before every question. Outlier only knows what you type into it.
  • Cross-app retrieval. Enterprise Search spans Slack, Google Drive, GitHub, Microsoft Teams, and Jira, so one question can reach a thread, a spec, and a ticket in a single pass instead of three tab-switches. Outlier reads only what you paste into the box; building that connector-by-connector would take years.
  • Agents that run while you're asleep. Custom Agents fire on schedules and triggers on Notion's infrastructure. An on-device app can't run a 6 a.m. brief with the Mac closed — that's the physics of local AI.
  • Multiplayer output. Results land where teammates already work. Outlier's land in one person's window.
  • AI Meeting Notes. Transcription in 16 languages, speaker labeling (English only), mobile capture that survives switching apps, no bot joining the call. Outlier doesn't transcribe at all.
  • Compliance and reach. SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA with a BAA on Enterprise, plus Windows, iOS and Android clients, a REST API, and an MIT-licensed MCP server. If procurement needs a BAA, or half your team is on Windows, Outlier isn't in the running.

What running locally actually buys you

The content never leaves. Notion's default of not training on customer data is a policy backed by contracts, and I've no reason to doubt it — but it's still a promise about what a third party does with content you sent them. With Outlier there's no request to make a promise about. That gap widens below Enterprise, where Notion's docs put provider retention at up to 30 days rather than zero.

It works with the network off. Notion's offline mode covers viewing and editing cached pages; anything needing a live connection is unavailable, and Notion documents no on-device model and lists no AI feature as offline-capable. On a plane, behind a restrictive firewall, or during a provider outage, Outlier keeps generating — the weights are on your SSD.

The cost stops moving. Notion AI comes with Business, but the automation layer is metered on top: Custom Agents have consumed Notion Credits since May 4, 2026 at $10 per 1,000, and Workers begin requiring credits on October 15, 2026 at roughly $0.0023 per run. Notion's published per-run estimates span a 10× range — $0.03–$0.11 for a Q&A agent, $0.10–$0.30 for a daily brief. Not expensive; just variable.

And it's good enough for the writing half. Summarize this, tighten this, pull the action items out of these notes — a local 27B handles that. In a 54-prompt head-to-head against Claude Opus, Outlier's Core 27B matched on 98.9% of rubric checks, including all nine of the hardest prompts. On a blind slice of SWE-bench Verified it measured about 45% (18/40) — respectable, clearly not frontier.

Notion AI, a local model, or both

Stay with Notion AI if your team runs on Notion, you need output in a shared space, you want scheduled agents, you rely on transcription or cross-app search, you're on Windows or Linux or mobile, or your org needs the compliance paperwork. Nothing local replaces that stack.

Add a local model if some slice of your work can't be sent to a third party at all, you want AI on a plane or behind a firewall, you're one operator paying a per-seat price for a single seat, or you'd rather have a fixed cost than a metered one.

Run both, which is what I'd expect most people to do. Notion AI for the collaborative, connected, scheduled work; a local model for the documents that shouldn't leave the building.
