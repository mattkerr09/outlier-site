Phi-4-mini scores 66 on MMLU at 2.9 GB — the best small model we measured, but not at arithmetic

Outlier · solo-built in Grand Rapids · published 2026-09-15 Last updated 2026-09-15
Quick answer
  • Phi-4-mini-instruct (4-bit, 2.9 GB) scored 66 on MMLU — the highest of the small models we measured, and 14 points above the 52 we recorded for the 4B we were shipping.
  • Qwen3-4B is the arithmetic pick: 56 on MMLU but 94 on GSM8K, and it is smaller at 2.3 GB.
  • Qwen2.5-3B scored 56. Against Phi-4-mini’s 66 that is a 10-point gap — and at n=50 our interval is about ±10, so we are not calling it a win.
  • Pick on the job, not the leaderboard: knowledge → Phi-4-mini. Word problems and the smallest footprint → Qwen3-4B.

Small models are where local AI is actually decided. Anything in the 3–4 billion parameter range fits in a couple of gigabytes, runs on a base-model Mac without touching swap, and answers fast enough to feel native. The awkward part is that they are close enough to each other that most comparisons you will read are reporting noise with a confident face. So here are ours, with the sample size attached, including the comparison we refuse to call.

The gap we will not call, and why that matters more than the one we will

At n=50 the confidence interval on an MMLU score is roughly ±10 points. That single fact reorganises the whole table. Phi-4-mini at 66 against Qwen2.5-3B at 56 is a ten-point gap sitting exactly on the edge of that interval, which means the honest answer is we cannot separate them at this sample size — not “Phi-4-mini wins by ten”.

The comparison that does survive is Phi-4-mini’s 66 against the 52 we measured for the 4B model we were shipping. Fourteen points clears the interval, and that is the one we acted on.

This is the part almost every small-model comparison leaves out. A benchmark number without a sample size cannot tell you whether its gap is real, and in this size class the gaps are usually smaller than the noise. If you take one thing from this page, take that.

Why the ranking flips on maths

Qwen3-4B loses on MMLU and wins on GSM8K by a distance — 94. MMLU is a broad multiple-choice knowledge test; GSM8K is grade-school word problems that require carrying a calculation through several steps without losing the thread. They measure different things, and a model tuned hard for one will not automatically lead on the other.

For a local assistant, which one matters depends entirely on what you ask it. “What year did the Treaty of Westphalia end the Thirty Years’ War” is a knowledge question. “If I buy three at £4.50 and the VAT is 20%, what do I owe” is an arithmetic question, and the model with the better general knowledge will cheerfully get it wrong.

How we know these are not harness artefacts

Because we produced the artefact first and recognised it. Running a custom-architecture model through direct MLX with Qwen3’s no-think token returns scores around 25. On a four-option multiple-choice benchmark, 25 is chance. A score at chance is not a weak model; it is a broken harness — the template is wrong, the model never sees a well-formed question, and it guesses. Read as a result it would have condemned a perfectly good model.

So those tiers are measured through the same sidecar path the app serves them on. Every number above was taken the way the model actually runs for a user, which is the only version of the number worth publishing.

How to decide

  • What do you actually ask it? Explanations, definitions, general recall → Phi-4-mini. Sums, unit conversions, multi-step word problems → Qwen3-4B.
  • How tight is your RAM? 2.3 GB against 2.9 GB is not much in isolation, but on a 8 GB Mac with a browser open it is the difference between resident and swapping.
  • Is the gap you are choosing on bigger than the noise? If a comparison does not state its sample size, treat its ranking as unproven — including ties you might have called wins.

Frequently asked questions

Is Phi-4-mini better than Qwen3-4B?

On general knowledge, yes, and by a margin we trust: 66 against 56 on MMLU at n=50. On arithmetic the order flips — Qwen3-4B scores 94 on GSM8K, which is the benchmark that actually predicts whether a small model can handle a word problem. Qwen3-4B is also smaller, 2.3GB against 2.9GB. So it is not one model being better; it is two different jobs.

Is a 10-point MMLU gap between two small models meaningful?

Often not, and ours is a good example. At n=50 the confidence interval is roughly plus or minus 10 points, so Phi-4-mini at 66 against Qwen2.5-3B at 56 sits right at the edge of the noise and we will not call it a win. The gap we do stand behind is Phi-4-mini's 66 against the 52 we measured for the 4B model we were shipping: 14 points, beyond the interval.
