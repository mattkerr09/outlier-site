Finance and accounting run on confidential data: client books, deal models, anything that counts as material non-public information. Cloud AI tools want all of it sitting on their servers. Local AI keeps it on your Mac instead. Outlier runs the models on-device, so sensitive financial data is never transmitted to a third-party AI vendor.

Why confidential financial data shouldn't go to cloud AI

Client financial records. Deal models. Audit workpapers. MNPI. This is exactly the data nobody should be pasting into a consumer chatbot. And it goes past confidentiality agreements. Firms in regulated contexts carry recordkeeping and information-barrier obligations that a cloud round-trip quietly complicates.

So a lot of teams settle on the safe default: no AI for anything client-sensitive. Which means handing back real productivity on analysis and drafting.

How local AI keeps the data in your control

Run the model on your own Mac and the data simply never leaves. Outlier does all the processing locally. Conversations sit on your disk, and the whole thing works with the network switched off. No third-party server is holding your spreadsheets. No vendor terms decide what happens to them.

What it does for finance work

  • Walking through spreadsheet logic and sanity-checking the formulas
  • Drafting memos, commentary, and narrative that's ready for a client
  • Boiling down filings, contracts, and long reports
  • Reasoning through reconciliation and data cleanup
  • Reading scanned statements with the Vision model

One rule: always verify the numbers. Language models can and do miscalculate. Lean on it to speed up the analysis you're going to check anyway, not to spit out figures you trust blind.

What people in finance ask most

Can I use AI on client financial data?

With local AI, yes — because the data stays on your Mac and is never sent to a third-party vendor. Confirm it fits your firm's confidentiality and recordkeeping policies. Verify any numbers the model produces.

Does it handle spreadsheets?

It reasons about spreadsheet logic, explains formulas, and drafts commentary from data you paste in. It is not a calculator — always check computed figures against your source.
